00Opener

I'm a payments product manager with 8+ years, about seven of them in payments, across a bank, a national payment rail and a technology services firm.

At Kotak I worked on the acquiring and payment gateway side. I automated daily merchant payment reporting, from 9 hours to 1.5, and owned the e-commerce chargeback book end to end. At NPCI Bharat Billpay I spent three years on the rail itself. I took the National Pension System live on Bharat Connect, coordinating the recordkeeping agencies, the trustee bank and the app partners under PFRDA, and I was on the core team for Banking Connect, the interoperable net-banking platform that banks and payment aggregators adopted.

Today at NeoSOFT I'm the sole product owner on a reconciliation platform for a digital insurer, where I chose the banking partner and the payment gateway and took it to pilot in 90 days.

Your stack is acquiring, payouts, escrow and banking connectivity sold to banks. I have worked in that space from three sides: the bank, the rail and the integrator.

01The company

  • Omniqe builds bank-ready payment and banking infrastructure for banks, NBFCs, payment aggregators and fintechs.
  • Four product lines. Acquiring: Acquira (POS and UPI QR), Omnigate (white-label gateway and routing), Paylinqe (payment links). Disbursement and collections: Swiftniqe (payouts over UPI, IMPS, NEFT, RTGS), OmniVAN (virtual accounts), Vaultom (escrow). Connectivity: Omniconnect (banking APIs and routing), OmniHub (corporate banking), OmniVAR (partner-bank referrals and switch work). Risk intelligence.
  • They sell the technology. The money sits with the partner bank or a trustee, so the product role lives between bank partners, merchants and compliance.

02The role

  • End-to-end product lifecycle: discovery, PRDs and BRDs, roadmap, launch and post-launch.
  • Acquiring, gateways, payouts, collections, escrow, settlement and reconciliation.
  • Transaction flows from authorisation to settlement, including refunds, reversals and exceptions.
  • Integrations with banks, payment aggregators, gateways and NPCI networks.
  • Success rates, operational issues and product metrics. UAT, releases and RCA.
  • Regulatory, risk, security and audit alignment in the Indian payments system.

05Likely questions

QuestionMy angle
Walk me through a payment from tap to settlementAuthorisation through the acquirer, network and switch, then the settlement file, the merchant credit net of MDR, and reconciliation against the bank statement. Exceptions: unmatched, partial, duplicate, reversed.
How do you design reconciliation?Two-way first, bank against ledger, then three-way with the gateway or policy system. Matching keys, tolerance rules, auto-allocation, suspense and an ageing exception queue. Track the auto-match rate.
How would you pick a banking partner or a gateway?Success rate by payment mode, settlement cycle and cut-offs, API quality and sandbox, onboarding time, commercials, support and compliance posture.
What goes wrong with payouts?Beneficiary validation, IMPS limits and failures, NEFT and RTGS windows, deemed success and late reversals, and idempotency so a retry never pays twice.
Escrow and virtual accountsEscrow: funds held by the bank or trustee, released on conditions with maker-checker approval and an audit trail. Virtual accounts: each payer gets a unique number, so every credit identifies and reconciles itself.
Which of our products would you improve?Pick Omniconnect or OmniVAN. Ask who the buyer is at the bank and the success metric, then suggest a success-rate and failover view per bank partner.
Why this move?To own a payments product end to end. Omniqe builds the exact stack I have worked on.
