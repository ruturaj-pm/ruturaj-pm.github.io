Opening questions

1. Tell me about yourself

Tests: can you be brief and land on a point. About 90 seconds, then stop.

I am a payments product manager with 8+ years of experience. The thread through it is the money layer under a product. That is the part that has to be right.

Three of those years were on the operator side at Kotak Mahindra Bank, in digital payments operations for the bank's acquiring and payment gateway business. I owned the ecommerce acquiring dispute book end to end. Issuer chargeback in, evidence assembled, representment filed, settlement adjusted. I also rebuilt the daily merchant payment reporting workflow. Processing time fell from 9 hours to 1.5 hours, accuracy rose from 96% to 99.98%, and we had zero non-technical errors for 12 months in a row.

Then three years at NPCI Bharat Billpay, on national payment rails. I shipped the National Pension System contribution flow into a 15M+ subscriber base. I also contributed to Banking Connect, India's first interoperable net-banking platform, including its settlement and dispute-management processes across 10+ partner banks.

Now I am at NeoSOFT. I was sole product owner on a reconciliation platform for a pre-launch digital general insurer: banking integration, receipting, and two-way and three-way reconciliation. Zero to pilot in 90 days.

So I bring acquiring disputes from one side and national rails from the other. Acquiring risk at a scaling fintech is exactly where the two join.

Depth questions

4. Walk me through your chargeback experience

Tests: the depth behind the chargeback line. Answer with the full lifecycle.

This was ecommerce acquiring, so card not present. The issuer raises a chargeback. It lands with us with a reason code and a deadline. The first decision is accept or defend, and that is a commercial call. Defending a small dispute can cost more than the loss.

If we defend, I assemble the evidence. The authorisation, proof of delivery or fulfilment, the merchant's own records, and the AVS and CVV result. The representment is filed inside the scheme window. Then it either sticks, or the issuer takes it to pre-arbitration and the economics change again.

Then comes settlement adjustment, the step people forget. The money has already gone to the merchant. So recovery is a debit against their next settlement. If the balance is not there, you are into negative balance and real loss.

5. How would you reduce losses where the chargeback lands after settlement?

Tests: can you think in controls across a timeline. This is a line from the job description, so it is a likely question.

I would frame it as three windows. The earliest intervention is always the cheapest.

WindowControls
Before settlementThis is where the real money is. Score merchant risk at onboarding and keep scoring after. Use delayed settlement or a rolling reserve for the risky tail. Add velocity and pattern rules at transaction time. Hold funds on the right 1% of merchants and the loss never happens.
At dispute timeUse prevention alerts so the merchant can refund before it becomes a chargeback. A refund costs less than a chargeback fee plus the hit to the scheme count. Then improve representment quality. That is mostly an evidence-completeness problem, and it can be automated.
AfterRecover against future settlement. Handle negative balances automatically, as soon as they appear.

On day one I would want the loss curve broken down by merchant cohort and reason code. My guess is that it is highly concentrated. If it is, the answer is a reserve policy, and a detection model can wait.

6. What metrics would you own?

Tests: do you see the metrics as a trade-off.

  • Fraud loss rate, in basis points of volume.
  • Chargeback rate, against scheme thresholds.
  • Recovery rate.
  • False positive rate.
  • Operations: cost per case, handling time and manual review effort.

The pair I would actively manage is the pair that fight each other: fraud loss rate and false positive rate. Blocking everything drives fraud to zero and kills the business. The job is choosing where on that curve to sit. That is a commercial decision, made jointly with Risk and Finance.

7. Card present versus card not present

Tests: vocabulary and clarity about scope.

My acquiring depth is ecommerce, which is card not present. I know EMV shifts the liability model and that card present fraud is physical. Card present is the side I would be learning.

8. Where would Agentic AI actually help here?

Tests: product judgement. The job description says automation should go where manual review adds cost without improving the outcome.

Case handling, before the decision. The decision itself needs a model or a rule you can explain to a regulator. The expensive part is everything around it. Pulling the transaction history, the merchant profile, prior disputes and the evidence bundle, then writing the summary. That is hours of analyst time with no risk judgement in it.

So I would target evidence assembly for representment and case preparation for review, with the analyst making the call. That is the shape I built at NeoSOFT. The model does the work, quality gates flag problems, and a person reviews only the exceptions.

The design choice I would defend is keeping the human gate. A pipeline that can make up a fact is worse than no pipeline. In a risk function, a wrong summary that nobody checks becomes a wrong decision with money attached.

Tests: the actual job. Very likely, since the team is new.
