00Opener

90-second pitch

"I'm a payments product manager with 8+ years across banking and payments infrastructure.

I spent three years at Kotak in payment-gateway operations. Merchant settlements, reconciliation, fee and MDR analytics. That is where I learned controls and process discipline.

Then I spent over three years at NPCI Bharat Billpay. I led the biller analytics platform. Then, as product manager, I launched the National Pension System on Bharat Connect. That meant coordinating three central recordkeeping agencies, the trustee bank and the regulator. That work was programme management as well as product: dependencies, steering cadence, UAT gates, go-live.

At NeoSOFT I built a greenfield reconciliation platform for a digital insurer, and I'm building compliance tooling now.

PMO in your risk-tech context is the muscle I've used at national scale. I want to apply it inside one BFSI franchise."

05Likely questions

QuestionMy angle
Why PMO when you are a PM?"Both roles deliver outcomes through others. A large part of my NPCI product work was programme orchestration across banks, recordkeeping agencies and a regulator. PMO in risk-tech uses that skill where the stakes are controls and compliance. That is where this company's transformation lives."
A programme is slipping. What do you do?Re-baseline against the critical path. Isolate the blocking dependency. Escalate with options and a recommendation. Protect the go-live gate.
Example: the NPS launch dependencies.
How do you report?One tracker as the single source of truth. RAG status with set thresholds. A one-page steering pack.
Example: I have built MIS surfaces myself. The Biller Analytics Dashboard runs at about 50K monthly users. My dashboards are working products.
What is your risk and compliance exposure?Controls and RCAs. Launches that clear a regulator. Reconciliation builds.
Example: Kotak reconciliation and settlement controls. NPCI launches. The NeoSOFT insurer reconciliation platform.
How do you handle team or stakeholder friction?Influence without authority.
Example: NPS, with three central recordkeeping agencies with competing priorities, a bank and PFRDA. Kept aligned through weekly steering and a shared dependency log.
