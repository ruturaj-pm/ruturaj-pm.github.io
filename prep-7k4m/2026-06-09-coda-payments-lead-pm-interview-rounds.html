The process

Four rounds. Rounds 1 to 3 are with Principal Product Managers. Each one is a gate, and the depth rises each round. Round 4 is the final, with the VP of Product.

The role sits in one of two product teams. The Payments team covers payment integration, partner integration and B2B payment execution. The Commerce team covers growth, with a mandate to grow publisher revenue.

RoundWithLikely focus
1Principal PMBreadth, plus a live case.
2Principal PMDeeper execution. A harder case or a deep dive on a past product.
3Principal PMData and numerical pressure. "A product you shipped end to end." Use the NPS launch or Biller Analytics.
4VP of ProductJudgment under ambiguity, business sense, conviction backed by data, and altitude. Think team and roadmap, above single features.

Every round can test four things: problem solving and execution, use of data, numerical thinking and a live case study.

The money equation

Net revenue = GMV x take rate x payment success rate, minus (PSP cost + chargeback and fraud loss + FX and settlement cost)

Every case moves one of these terms. Find the lever the case is asking about. Say it out loud first.

The six-step case spine

Takes 5 to 8 minutes. State the spine before diving in.

StepTimeWhat to do
1. Clarify30 to 60 sAsk 2 or 3 sharp questions. Which surface: Codashop, Codapay or Custom Commerce? Which market? What is the goal metric and the constraint? Is it a live case or hypothetical?
2. Frame and decompose1 minRestate the goal as a metric. Draw the driver tree or the loss buckets out loud.
3. Prioritize1 minPick 1 or 2 levers by impact and effort, or RICE. Say why the others were dropped. That is the judgment signal.
4. Solution2 to 3 minConcrete actions on the chosen lever. Trade-offs. Name the risk and the mitigation.
5. Metrics and validation1 minNorth star plus guardrail. How to A/B test or pilot. Kill criteria.
6. Risks and next steps30 sWhat could break. What I would do on Monday morning.

Under time pressure, protect steps 1 to 3. "In the interest of time, I will go deep on the top lever and list the rest." Think aloud and narrate trade-offs. Ask for 60 to 90 seconds to structure. Revising a number mid-answer is fine.

Case frameworks

Case A: Grow a publisher from $50M to $100M (Commerce)

Decompose: Revenue = Players x Payer conversion x Purchase frequency x Average order value x Payment success rate x Take rate.

  • Payer conversion: local payment methods, a lower minimum top-up, a first-purchase incentive. Often the biggest unlock.
  • Payment success: local methods, smart retries, fewer steps. It is pure margin, so lead here.
  • Purchase frequency: retention loops, top-up reminders, voucher rails.
  • Average order value: bundles, price tiers by purchasing power, promo timing.
  • New markets and methods: last, once the existing funnel is fixed.

Line: "First I would find where the $50M is leaking. Pull the cohort funnel by market and method. Find the segments with high traffic and low success or low conversion, and concentrate there. Adding 10 points to a broken funnel beats entering a new market cold."

Proof: Biller Analytics. 20,000+ billers, about 50K monthly active users, 150x growth in report downloads. I have built the diagnostic surface this case needs.

Case B: Improve payment success rate across markets

  • Coverage gap: add the local methods players want.
  • Technical declines such as timeouts and PSP downtime: redundancy, smart routing, retries with backoff.
  • Risk and fraud false positives: tune thresholds per market. Step up checks only on high-risk payments.
  • UX drop-off: fewer steps, saved methods, local currency and copy.

Line: "Success rate breaks into four buckets: coverage, technical, risk and UX. Measure each per market and per PSP, then fix the largest bucket. Each market is hyper-local, so route per market."

Proof: Kotak acquiring operations and the merchant payment reporting redesign, 9 hours to 1.5 hours at 99.98% accuracy. I have run the back end that proves a success number turned into settled money.

Case C: Launch a new payment method or market

  • Demand sizing: a live back-of-envelope estimate.
  • Economics: carrier billing can cost 10 to 15% or more, against roughly 1 to 3% for cards and wallets. Add FX. Check take rate net of cost. Is the method margin-accretive or volume-accretive?
  • Integration: direct or through an aggregator. The settlement and reconciliation flow. Refunds and chargebacks.
  • Risk and compliance: fraud profile, KYC, licensing.
  • Go-to-market: which titles first. Default or optional method.
  • Instrumentation and rollback: success target, reconciliation match target, kill criteria.

Line: "A payment method is a product with its own profit and loss. I only ship a method I can reconcile."

Proof: The National Pension System launch on Bharat Connect. The first investment-cum-savings category on the platform. Three central recordkeeping agencies, the trustee bank, the technology service provider and the front-end app partners, all under PFRDA. Live across a 15M+ subscriber base.

Case D: Reduce settlement and reconciliation friction across publishers (Payments)

  • Ingestion: many PSPs, methods, currencies and file formats. Normalize them first.
  • Matching: two-way matches gateway against internal records. Three-way adds the bank. Anything unmatched becomes an exception.
  • Exception handling: who clears it, how fast, and how much is automated.
  • Settlement to the publisher: FX, fee netting, payout timing, clear statements.
  • Disputes and chargebacks: representment and write-back to the ledger.

Levers: auto-match rate (the headline), exception TAT, settlement cycle (T+n) and self-serve settlement statements.

Line: "Reconciliation friction comes down to three numbers: auto-match rate, exception TAT and days to settle. Instrument those three. Attack the largest exception category, such as FX rounding, partial refunds or timing. Give publishers a self-serve statement so they stop raising tickets that ask where their money is."

Proof: Kotak chargebacks, representment and settlement discrepancy work. Settlement and dispute processes on Banking Connect. The two-way and three-way reconciliation build at NeoSOFT. This is the problem I have spent the most time on, across three roles.

Case E: Onboard publishers faster (Commerce)

  • Map the funnel: lead, KYC and compliance, integration, testing, go-live. Find the longest stage and the stage with the highest drop.
  • Attack TAT stage by stage: self-serve KYC, a sandbox, better SDK and docs, templated integration, pre-built connectors.
  • Segment: white-glove service for top publishers, self-serve for the long tail.
  • North star: time to first transaction. That is the point where revenue starts.

Proof: onboarding automation at NPCI Bharat Billpay cut onboarding TAT from 90 days to 15 days, about 83% less. Lead with the 83%.

Numerical drills

DrillWorking
1. Size a mobile-game top-up market for a region of about 600M people.600M people. About 70% online gives 420M. About 60% of those play mobile games, so 250M. About 6% pay, so 15M payers. At about $70 a year each, that is roughly $1B a year. Give a range of $0.75B to $1.5B. The least certain input is payer conversion. Say how to validate it.
2. Payment success lift.$50M captured at 92% success means about $54.3M attempted. At 95%, 54.3 x 0.95 is about $51.6M. That is +$1.6M, about +3.3%, at near-zero marginal cost. This is the case for success rate ahead of a new market.
3. Take rate and cost lever (illustrative inputs).Assume $4B volume at a blended take rate of about 12%. That is about $480M net revenue. A 1-point cut in blended payment cost on $4B is $40M straight to contribution. A board-level lever.
4. Onboarding throughput.1,000 long-tail publishers, a 90-day TAT, a team of 5. Self-serve at 15 days cuts TAT about 6x. That gives about 6x throughput on the same team, or frees 4 of the 5 people for strategic accounts. Ties to the 90-to-15-day result.

Estimation habits, said out loud: assumptions first. Round numbers. Sanity-check against a known anchor. Give a range and name the assumption to validate. End with the decision the number drives.

Stories

1. Reconciliation platform, zero to pilot in 90 days (NeoSOFT)

  • Situation: sole product owner on a banking-integration and reconciliation platform for a pre-launch digital general insurer. No existing system. A hard 90-day pilot target.
  • Task: design and ship two-way and three-way reconciliation across in-house receipting, bank statements and the gateway ledger. Plus the banking and payment-gateway integration underneath.
  • Action: selected the banking partner and the payment gateway. Owned the partner integration specs end to end. Sequenced integration so the reconciliation logic was tested against real flows early. Scoped two-way first, receipts against bank. Added the gateway leg for three-way once the rails were stable.
  • Result: pilot shipped inside 90 days, ahead of the client's own launch.
  • Link to Coda: the closest match to building Coda's payment-integration and settlement layer. Greenfield, partner specs, reconciliation at the center, on a tight clock.

2. Banking Connect, India's first interoperable net-banking platform (NPCI Bharat Billpay)

  • Situation: an RBI-initiated rollout to let corporates pay across many banks on standard rails.
  • Task: as a core team contributor, help define standard payment flows, settlement and dispute processes, and the Lien Marking and Mandate framework.
  • Action: worked on standard payment and settlement flows, dispute-management design and the Lien Marking and Mandate framework. Heavy multi-bank coordination. Each integration had its own quirks to bring to one standard.
  • Result: interoperable net banking live across 10+ partner banks, adopted by top banks and payment aggregators. A 0-to-1 platform in production.
  • Link to Coda: Codapay connects publishers to many payment methods. Bringing many partners onto one standard flow is the problem I worked on here.

3. Merchant payment reporting and a pricing anomaly (Kotak Mahindra Bank)

  • Situation: in digital payments operations, merchant payment reporting was manual, slow and error-prone. 9-hour cycles at 96% accuracy. Fee logic that nobody watched closely.
  • Task: make the data fast and trustworthy enough to decide on. Use it to catch what manual work missed.
  • Action: redesigned and automated the daily reporting workflow for the acquiring and payment-gateway business. Built Excel and Power BI dashboards on fee management. The dashboards surfaced pricing anomalies.
  • Result: processing time from 9 hours to 1.5 hours, 83% less. Accuracy from 96% to 99.98%. Zero non-technical errors for 12 months in a row. The pricing anomalies saved about INR 1.9 million a year.
  • Link to Coda: the rounds weigh data and numerical thinking heavily. Build the dashboard, find the anomaly, size the impact and let the number drive the call.

4. Self-serve analytics for 20,000+ billers (NPCI Bharat Billpay)

  • Situation: billers on Bharat Connect had zero visibility into their collection performance.
  • Task: build the first biller-facing analytics product, useful enough that billers keep coming back.
  • Action: designed a self-serve dashboard for 20,000+ billers across 30 categories. Volume, value, technical and business decline rates, complaint counts. Billers could diagnose their own performance.
  • Result: about 50K monthly active users with about 30-minute average sessions. Report downloads grew from 100 in month 1 to about 15,000 by month 12, a 150x rise.
  • Link to Coda: the Commerce team's mandate is growth. Self-serve product raises revenue per merchant without adding account managers at the same rate.

5. Onboarding time from 90 days to 15 (NBBL Hackathon 2025)

  • Situation: onboarding Agent Institutions and Operating Units onto Bharat Connect took about 90 days. That capped network growth.
  • Task: cut onboarding time without adding operations headcount.
  • Action: built end-to-end onboarding automation, paired with a small language model compliance assistant trained on 2 years of NBBL circulars. It automated the document, verification and compliance-check steps.
  • Result: TAT from 90 days to 15 days, about 83% less. Won the NBBL Hackathon 2025.
  • Link to Coda: for a Commerce team adding publishers all the time, onboarding speed is a direct growth lever.

6. National Pension System launch, for stakeholder questions (NPCI Bharat Billpay)

  • Category manager for the first investment-cum-savings category on Bharat Connect, launched October 2024.
  • Aligned three central recordkeeping agencies, the trustee bank, the technology service provider and the front-end app partners under PFRDA. Each had its own compliance gates and timelines.
  • Delivered a 5-click contribution flow with mobile-number-linked PRAN authentication, replacing 12-digit PRAN memorisation.
  • Live across a 15M+ subscriber base. The best answer to "a product you shipped end to end".

Domain notes: settlement and reconciliation

TermMeaning
Merchant of RecordThe entity that sells to the end customer and owns the payment. It therefore owns reconciliation, settlement and the books.
Two-way reconciliationMatch two sources, such as internal records against the gateway or the bank.
Three-way reconciliationAdd a third source, so internal records, the gateway and the bank all agree.
ExceptionA transaction that fails to match. It needs an owner and a TAT.
Auto-match rateThe share of transactions matched with no human touch. The headline reconciliation metric.
Settlement cycle (T+n)Days from transaction to funds reaching the publisher.
Fee nettingDeducting fees from the payout before it goes to the publisher. Statements must show it clearly.
Chargeback and representmentA chargeback is a disputed payment reversed by the issuer. Representment is the merchant's evidence-backed challenge. The flow ends in a settlement adjustment.
Confirmed versus settledKeep "transaction confirmed" and "funds settled" as separate states. Mismatches then surface fast, while they are still small.
